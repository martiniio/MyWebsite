# About Me

I'm an Electrical and Computer Engineer. I work where hardware meets software — firmware that drives silicon, RTL that implements protocols, control systems that have to meet deadlines.

What keeps me here is that the constraints are real. A sensor has a settling time. A bus has a setup margin. A control loop has a deadline. None of them negotiate.

Most of my work is in safety-critical environments, automotive and industrial and adjacent domains. In that world the process is part of the engineering, not overhead on top of it. Requirements get traced. Edge cases get written down. Interfaces get specified before anyone implements them.

## What I'm Interested In

- **Embedded firmware** — bare metal and RTOS, control loops, drivers, communication stacks
- **State machines** — explicit designs that are easy to reason about
- **FPGA and RTL** — Verilog, VHDL, interface logic, formal methods
- **Robotics** — motion control, sensing, the messy boundary where software meets the physical world
- **Safety-critical systems** — ISO 26262, IEC 61508
- **Requirements engineering** — capture, trace, validate
- **Real-time systems** — FreeRTOS, Zephyr, QNX

## Why This Blog
I write about the process, not just the result — the decisions, the trade-offs, the dead ends and my learing journey. A working prototype hides the two hundred choices that made it work, and those choices are the interesting part.

Small and large are the same activity at different scales. A [rivet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivet) is a metal pin with a soft head, hammered flat on the other side. That is the whole idea. Millions of them hold every airliner in the sky. **The wing is nothing more than a shape made of rivets that do not let go.**
